Section two

Who pays for the default?

A default is a decision you make on behalf of everyone who will never open the settings screen. Most of them never will. So the default is not a convenience — it is the shape the product actually takes in the world.

When we argue about a feature, we argue about the version we would choose ourselves: the informed user, the careful reader, the person with time. That person is rare. The real user arrives tired, mid-task, on a borrowed device, and takes whatever we put in front of them. The default is the product.

Ask the question plainly. If ninety-five people out of a hundred keep this setting exactly as it ships, what have we chosen for them? And who absorbs the difference between what is easiest for us to build and what would be best for them to receive?

The cost never disappears. It only moves to someone with less power to refuse it.
Practice About one hour
Trace one default in something you have shipped.
01 Name the default and the meeting where it was set. Write down who was in the room.
02 Estimate how many people live with it and never change it. A rough number is enough.
03 Write one sentence you would be willing to say to them out loud about why it ships this way.
