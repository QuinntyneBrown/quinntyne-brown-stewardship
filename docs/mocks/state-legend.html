Marks, contrast, and targets

This sheet is normative for every other mock. It records what each mark means, which palette role may carry text and at what size, and how focus and hit areas are drawn — so that no state is carried by lightness alone and no text falls below its contrast floor.

Shape carries the state, not lightness: a filled disc with a check, an amber disc inside a halo that is visibly larger than any other mark, and a hollow ring. Every marker also carries its state word for assistive technology, and the curriculum prints the key beneath the path.

Focus and target size

A single ring cannot clear 3:1 against both the amber action and the page ground, so the ring is two-tone — a dark inner band, then a light outer one.

A focused field
Every interactive element sits in a box of at least 44 by 44, even where the drawn mark is smaller — the dashed line is the target, the ring is what you see. Adjacent hit areas never overlap. Inline links inside running prose are exempt.
